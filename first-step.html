Case study

First Step

Turning "work on my portfolio" into something you can start in 10 minutes.

Career-focused students don't stall because they're lazy. They stall because their tasks are too vague to start. First Step breaks a vague task down the moment you add it and shows you one step a day. That first step is guaranteed to take 10 minutes or less.

01 · Problem and research

The big stuff kept sitting there

I kept writing tasks too vague to start, like "work on portfolio." So I'd knock out the small, easy tasks instead and feel productive while the important work sat untouched. It happened a couple of times a week and led to rushed work, missed opportunities and stress.

Before building anything, I ran a personal workflow: an AI turned each vague task into one first step, I time-blocked it, and it came back in a daily morning brief. It worked for me. The question was whether it would work for anyone else.

Who it's for

Career-oriented students with self-directed, future-facing work (recruiting, applications, side projects) and no one telling them what to do next. The problem is worst during unstructured stretches like school breaks.

02 · Design decisions

Every choice fights the urge to stall

1. Break it down at capture, not later

Decision: When you add a task, the AI asks one clarifying question and then writes the steps right away.

Why: The moment you write "apply to internships" is the moment you know the most about what you meant. Waiting until later is how vague tasks go stale.

2. Show one step a day

Decision: The Today tab shows a single step, never the full list.

Why: A list lets you pick the easiest item and call it progress, which is the exact behavior I was trying to fix. With one step, there's nothing to hide behind.

3. The first step always takes 10 minutes or less

Decision: This is the app's one hard rule. If the AI returns a first step longer than 10 minutes, the server has it split that step before the user ever sees it. Users can make the limit stricter (down to 2 minutes) but never looser.

Why: Getting started is the hardest part. I didn't want it to be a prompt suggestion the AI might ignore, so I made it a guarantee the product enforces.

4. Give people a way out that isn't quitting

Decision: Every step has three buttons: Done, Still too big (splits it into a first step of 5 minutes or less) and Not today.

Why: When a step feels too hard, people close the app. "Still too big" turns that moment into a smaller step instead of a lost user.

5. A factual stakes line

Decision: Each step can show a short, factual line about why it matters, with a setting to turn it off.

Why: A little urgency helps, but guilt makes people avoid the app. Keeping it factual and optional was the balance.

6. Keep the user in control

Decision: You can edit any step, regenerate the breakdown and choose how many steps a task gets.

Why: AI steps are a starting point, not orders. People trust the plan more when they can change it.

7. Breaking my own PRD: calendar time-blocking

Decision: My PRD scoped calendar features out. I added an "Add to Google Calendar" button anyway, and I'm updating the PRD to match.

Why: In my own workflow, turning a step into a time block was the real unlock. I kept it cheap: the button opens Google Calendar with the event already filled in, so the app didn't need a full calendar connection.

What I cut

  • How-it-works slides. I replaced them with a guided first task, so new users learn the app by using it on something real.
  • A full calendar integration. A prefilled Google Calendar link gets most of the value with none of the permission prompts.
  • The full task list on Today. It's still in the Tasks tab for when you need it, but it's never the default.
03 · Positioning and launch

How I'd bring it to market

"The work that moves you forward, one step at a time."

Positioning

For career-focused students who keep putting off the work that matters most, First Step is an AI task tool that turns any vague goal into one step you can start in 10 minutes. To-do apps show you everything at once. First Step shows you where to begin.

Messaging

  • Start, don't plan. You never face a blank page or a wall of tasks.
  • Small enough to say yes to. Every first step takes 10 minutes or less, guaranteed.
  • Built for the long game. It's for recruiting, applications and projects, not groceries.

Launch plan

  • Timing: launch at the start of recruiting season, when "apply to internships" is on every student's list.
  • Channels: career and consulting clubs, where one officer demo reaches a room of people with the exact problem.
  • Hook: a live demo that turns a vague task someone in the room shouts out into a 10-minute first step.
  • What I'd measure: the share of new users who finish their first step on day one, and how many come back to complete a second.
How it's built

Shipping it for real

First Step started as a Claude artifact. I rebuilt it as a real web app with Claude Code and shipped it.

  • Vercel hosts the app and deploys every change I push to GitHub.
  • Anthropic API writes the questions and steps. The API key and every prompt stay on the server, and the endpoint only accepts First Step's three requests, so nobody can use it for anything else.
  • Supabase handles sign-up (email or Google) and syncs tasks across devices. Row-level security means each user can only ever see their own data.
  • Works at every level of setup. With no AI key or database configured, the app still runs and saves in the browser, so a demo never breaks.
What's next

Where I'd take it

  • Morning brief: send today's step in a daily brief, the way my original workflow did.
  • Nudges on stalled tasks: notice when a task keeps getting "Not today" and offer to split it smaller.
  • Google Tasks sync: meet people inside the tools they already use.
